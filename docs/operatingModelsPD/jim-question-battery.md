# Jim's Question Battery

Status: v1.1
Prepared: 2026-07-06
Source: data-access discussions 06/24/2026 and 07/06/2026 (Otter transcripts; quotes verbatim, transcription artifacts kept as-spoken). The meeting opener (#12) predates the recording and comes from Dustin's own notes.
Use: grill any proposal against these before presenting. Jim grounds **downward** (show me the instance); my evaluation lens grounds **upward** (does it solve the class). Run both directions.

---

## The battery — in the order he deploys it

1. **Plain-words confirmation** — "Would it be fair to say that today some people do not have access to the data systems they need to do their job?"
   → Can I state the problem so simply a stranger could confirm or deny it?

2. **Reflect and check** — "One problem is people can't get access. The other problem is they can't discover... How am I doing?" / "Is that a fair statement?"
   → Can I enumerate the distinct problems without merging them? He will offer his own list and invite correction — be ready to actually correct it.

3. **The instance demand** — "What's a specific example of a case that we're trying to unblock with these fixes?"
   → Name a real person, blocked right now, on a real task. No named person = no problem.

4. **Urgency (triple-barreled)** — "When's the next time we think this is really gonna bite us? Or what's the biggest pain point for you and your team onboarding a new dev? Or is the data person that's working with Caitlin having a hard time figuring out where the bodies are buried?"
   → Three interchangeable openings of one hunt: find the concrete pain. Answer any barrel with a date, a trigger event, or a named struggling person. "Eventually" fails all three.

5. **Named-user probe** — "Who right now needs, or is thinking about needing access? Could we use that as an example?... or is that crazy of me?" / "Does Chris need access to data?" / "If Jamie was to kick up and start doing ADB, would she run into this issue?"
   → He tests the abstract problem against specific people until one sticks — and drops misses without ceremony: "It might not be. It's fine if it isn't. We just drop it." Match that: offer candidate instances cheaply, never defend a bad one.

6. **The wedge** — "What is the wedge problem... the wedge goal that we would use to explore the space, knowing that we want it to be reusable?"
   → Theme ≠ project. "Data democratization is like the theme, not the project." What's the smallest issue that forces the space open and stays reusable?

7. **The flip side** — "Can you tell the flip side version of this story now... the happy path after this is built?"
   → Narrate the solved world concretely: who does what, without whom.

8. **Sufficiency** — "Are there other examples you would give... or is that totally sufficient? Like, if we solve that, then we're addressing this kind of pain that this meeting is the expression of?"
   → Does the proposed fix cover the pain that convened us, or just a corner of it?

9. **Feedback speed** — "Is that going to come up and give value quickly enough that we will know if it's working or not?"
   → How fast does reality tell us we're wrong? Slow-feedback deliverables get challenged.

10. **Actor/action/time** — "Who's asking what, when?"
    → Kills abstract capabilities ("an agent that answers things") dead. Every capability needs an actor, an action, and a moment.

11. **Classification** — "So this is a knowledge management question..."
    → He renames the problem to its class. Pre-empt him: what class is this *really*? Am I naming the class or a symptom?

12. **Meeting meta (bookended)** — opens with "What's our definition of success for this meeting?" (pre-recording; from Dustin's notes) and closes with "What does good look like for this time spent discussing this?"
    → He runs the success question at BOTH ends: criteria before the discussion, audit after. Walk in with the answer to the opener already written; expect the closer to check the meeting against it.

13. **The closer** — "What's actionable from all of this discussion at this time?" / "If I can turn this into something I can track, I'm much more likely to do it."
    → Leave him a table: action, owner, falsifiable done-when.

14. **Self-check (he runs it on himself)** — "Am I thinking about this right?"
    → He invites refutation of his own framing. Be ready to push back for real; agreement-by-default wastes the invitation.

## From the first meeting (same voice)

- "What are actual real measurable KPIs?" — expects two or three, not twenty.
- "How would you fix it?" — direct, immediately after laying out his own framing.
- "I'd be curious, your reaction to this" — wants a position, not a summary.
- "Do you feel sure?" — commitment probe on a strategic claim.
- "If you want to reflect on that... share what, how we can" — he closes by assigning take-away reflection rather than forcing an in-room answer. The follow-up meeting opens where the reflection was supposed to land: arrive with the proposal.

## Pre-meeting self-grill (condensed)

Before walking in, have one-line answers to:

- [ ] My definition of success for the meeting itself — stated in the first five minutes, checkable at the close.
- [ ] The classes of problems in this discussion — named before he names them (the self-directed version of #11).
- [ ] The problem in one plain sentence a stranger could confirm or deny.
- [ ] A named person blocked right now.
- [ ] The date or event when it bites next.
- [ ] The wedge — and why it's reusable beyond the instance.
- [ ] The happy-path story, 30 seconds.
- [ ] The number that tells us it's working — and how fast it arrives.
- [ ] What I can hand him to track (action / owner / done-when).
